# Competitor notes — August 2026

The seven names from Peter's intake, what each does well and badly, and
what HARDT should copy, counter, or ignore. Peter's own read (via Michael, Aug 2026, verbatim): "He is a
wholesaler but I liked the simplicity, how all the faces and bios where
on there, the page organization etc. another wholesaler is
sellersnewday." Three takeaways: (1) what he values is simplicity,
faces-and-bios, and page organization — now implemented (team cards on
About, address-first funnel forms); (2) he identifies both HHG and
Sellers New Day as wholesalers, which makes "we don't assign contracts"
the direct counter to the two sites he rates most; (3) he wants Frances,
his VA, on the site as a team member — done, pending her details and
headshot.

Two of the seven could not be confidently identified online: "Central
Valley Home Buyers" (probably Central Valley REI, covered below) and
"Best Home Offer" (name too generic to pin down). Peter can point us at
the right sites if they matter.

## The one that matters most: Home Helpers Group (homehelpersgroup.com)

The dominant player in Kern. Since 2013, ~205 Google reviews at 4.9,
BBB A+ accredited, "top home cash buying team in Bakersfield" by review
count, 40+ city landing pages.

**Doing well:** review volume nobody nearby can match; testimonials
with names, dates and specifics (solar lease handled, fast closes);
BBB badge; clean three-step process.

**Doing badly:** messaging identical to every other cash buyer; zero
valuation transparency ("condition and market trends"); thin team
presence; no video; 40-link nav clutter; city pages are boilerplate.

**For HARDT:** we will not out-review them this year. The counters are
(1) get the first three real reviews live (Chuck, Robert, Eli — already
in motion), (2) keep doing what they can't fake: shown math, the
calculator, sourced county research, and the how-we've-helped stories.
Their city pages would fail the 40% rule; ours don't. That's the gap
Google and homeowners can both see.

## Sellers New Day (sellersnewday.com)

Statewide, veteran & first-responder owned, "Let us beat your other
offer."

**Doing well:** identity-based trust — the veteran/first-responder
framing does real work; broad situation coverage; heavy FAQ.

**Doing badly:** placeholder "as seen on" media buttons with nothing
behind them (actively damages trust); 6+ identical CTAs per page;
"Don't lose hope!" urgency tone; no team photos.

**For HARDT:** confirms founder-identity positioning works (ours is the
builder/BMX story). Their fake-badge section is a reminder: never ship
a trust signal that can't be clicked and verified.

## GG Homes / ibuySD (gghomes.com, ibuysd.com)

San Diego, since 2014-15, "1,000+ happy customers." Now heavily
agent-facing: pitching realtors on bringing them deals with commission
protection.

**Doing well:** flip showcases with project names; clear value prop for
their (agent) audience.

**Doing badly:** for an actual homeowner the site is thin — one
testimonial, no process timeline, no valuation method. The pivot to
courting agents means the seller experience is an afterthought.

**For HARDT:** their drift away from sellers is our opening in San
Diego. Our seller-first pages (stories, research, calculator) address
the audience they're neglecting.

## Central Valley REI (cenvalrei.com)

Bakersfield local, physical office on F Street.

**Doing well:** local-roots storytelling (Bakersfield Sound references,
"treat every seller like family"), named team members in testimonials,
warm tone. The best non-HHG local play.

**Doing badly:** no third-party verification at all (no BBB, no dated
reviews); emotional appeals substitute for data; no offer methodology.

**For HARDT:** local texture works — but ours is built from sourced
facts (recorder timelines, transfer tax, housing stock) rather than
vibes, which is more defensible and harder to copy.

## New Western (newwestern.com)

National marketplace: connects investors to off-market properties,
$17B sales, licensed agents in 30+ markets.

**For HARDT:** not a direct competitor — a foil. They are the
industrialised version of exactly what we're positioned against: the
seller talks to intermediaries, and the house is a unit in a pipeline.
Useful contrast for copy, no need to compete on their terms.

## Pattern across all of them (the whitespace HARDT owns)

Every competitor shares four weaknesses, and each is already a HARDT
strength:

1. **Valuation opacity.** Nobody shows how the number is built. We show
   the math and ship a calculator that argues against ourselves.
2. **Interchangeable copy.** Swap the logos and nobody could tell the
   sites apart. Our tone rule (bad news at the same volume) and Peter's
   verbatim lists are unfakeable.
3. **Doorway city pages.** Boilerplate with the town name swapped. Our
   matrix pages carry sourced local facts and pass the swap test.
4. **Zero educational depth.** No one explains NOD timelines, Prop 19,
   or tenant rules. Our resources hub is alone in the market.

Where they beat us today, honestly stated: **review volume** (HHG's 205
vs our one on the old Fluid profile), **BBB accreditation** (planned,
not started), and **real photography** (in Peter's phone, not on the
site). All three are execution, not strategy.
