San Bernardino · Selling a rental

Done being a landlord in San Bernardino County.

A decade of deferred maintenance between tenancies is normal here. We price it plainly. You don’t fix it, and nobody gets displaced.

Get my offer(707) 489-6236

The San Bernardino County rental we see most is a workhorse: bought affordably, rented continuously, maintained reactively. After ten or fifteen years the gap between rent-ready and market-ready has grown to a renovation, and the owner is being told to empty the house, spend the renovation, and list, all to reach buyers who’ll then inspect the ’60s wiring anyway.

Selling occupied skips the whole sequence. State just-cause law keeps termination genuinely costly; transferring the tenancy costs nothing. Lease, deposit and the housing-authority relationship (if it’s a Section 8 tenancy) all move through escrow, and your tenant’s Tuesday is undisturbed.

The local details

What’s specific to San Bernardino.

The housing stock

The valley floor tells its age in decades: prewar and postwar bungalows in central San Bernardino and older Redlands, 1960s–70s ranches through Highland and the mid-city, and 1980s-onward tract across Fontana and Rancho Cucamonga. The older half is where inspection findings cluster: galvanised supply lines, aluminium branch wiring from the late ’60s and ’70s, and Federal Pacific panels that insurers increasingly refuse outright.

Transfer tax

Documentary transfer tax is the standard $0.55 per $500 of price, and none of the cities we buy in (San Bernardino, Fontana, Rancho Cucamonga, Redlands, Highland) levy a city tax on top.

Recording a deed

The Assessor–Recorder–Clerk records at the Hall of Records in San Bernardino, publishes a fee calculator, and supports same-day e-recording through authorised submitters, which in an ordinary escrow means the deed records the day of closing. Budget for the state’s $75-per-document Building Homes and Jobs Act fee on non-sale recordings; it surprises people doing family transfers.

What kills deals here

In the High Desert (Phelan, Piñon Hills, the unincorporated fringe), water and waste are private: wells, septic tanks, and a county health department that wants a percolation test on file before a septic permit moves. Financed buyers routinely lose weeks to well tests and septic certifications, which is why cash matters more up there than down the hill. On the valley floor it’s the older-stock trio above (plumbing, wiring, panels) that resets buyers’ numbers at inspection.

Sources: SB County Assessor-Recorder-Clerk · SB County Environmental Health septic FAQ. Figures checked August 2026; we re-verify quarterly.

On the ground

Pricing the workhorse honestly

We assume era-typical systems rather than hoping: galvanised plumbing and aluminium-wiring-vintage electrics in the older valley stock, roofs by decade, panels by brand. Long tenancies mask condition: tenants report failures, not fatigue, so our number is built from what the era says is behind the walls, and we show that reasoning instead of discovering it dramatically after a contract is signed.

Bring the lease and the last tax bill to the first call. Everything else (the walkthrough inside legal notice rules, the deposit proration, the arrears conversation if there is one) is process we’ve run many times, and it lands gently on the person living there.

State rules (AB 1482) are the operative layer for most of the county’s older rental stock. The practical issue we see isn’t the law. It’s condition: rentals here often carry a decade of deferred maintenance, and we price that plainly instead of asking you to fix it between tenancies.

High Desert landlords, one extra note: a rental on well and septic adds infrastructure questions to the tenancy questions, and financed investors typically want both resolved before closing. We don’t need the well tested to a lender’s standard to commit. We test for our own information and carry the risk ourselves, which is usually the difference between a sale that closes and a listing that expires up there. Down the hill, the same commitment just means fewer contingencies and a date that holds.

Paper to gather when you’re ready: the lease or a note of the verbal terms, the deposit amount, and the last tax bill. Everything else (estoppel, prorations, the housing-authority transfer where one applies) is escrow’s routine, not yours.
